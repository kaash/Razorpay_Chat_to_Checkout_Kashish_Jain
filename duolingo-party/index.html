TimeChapterNarration
OpeningDuolingo has a hundred and fifty-three million monthly learners. But on a typical day, fifty-eight percent of them don't open the app. This is Duolingo Party: a family board game where every move is earned by a correct answer, in your own course.
The family and the problemRavi Iyer is fifty-four and learning English. His daughter Ananya is learning Spanish, and stopped nine days ago. His son Kabir is learning Japanese. All three use Duolingo, but each learns alone, and nobody notices when someone stops.
1 · Ravi starts a Party after his lessonStep one. Ravi finishes his English lesson, the moment he feels proudest. Right there, the app offers a Party, and he shares the link in the family WhatsApp group.
2 · Ananya joins from the family chatStep two. Ananya taps the link and joins. She plays in Spanish, her father in English, and Kabir in Japanese, on one board. Each of them gets one turn a day, and everyone can see who has played.
3 · Her turn: two exercises from her Spanish courseStep three. Her turn is two real exercises from her own Spanish course, at her level. A correct answer earns a roll of the die; a wrong answer earns nothing, and the word comes back in her next lesson. Her first roll is a two.
4 · Correct answers are rolls; she captures RaviStep four. She gets the second exercise right too. Her next roll lands on her father's piece and sends it back to start, the move every Ludo player knows.
5 · The turn counts as her active dayStep five. Her turn restarted her streak and earned two experience points, about a lesson's rate. It is an active day she would otherwise have skipped, and across millions of learners, days like this make up the lift.
6 · Next morning: one Party nudgeStep six. The next morning, Ravi gets one notification: his daughter knocked him back last night, and his turn for today is ready. Party sends at most one nudge a day, only about people he plays with.
7 · Sunday finish; Ravi invites his sisterStep seven. On Sunday evening, the family plays the finish live. The result card goes back to the family chat, and Ravi invites his sister Meera, who is new to Duolingo.
How this raises DAU, and the impactHere is how that moves daily active users. They equal monthly learners times active days, divided by thirty, so every turn on a day a learner would have skipped adds one active day. In the base case, about one in five monthly learners is still playing six months in. Family games alone add about seven percent. Open tables and invited relatives take it to ten point two percent, or six point six million more daily learners, with no ads, paid marketing, deals or subscriptions. The margin is thin, so the first test checks adoption.
Next steps: the family study and the 28-day testNext, thirty families play the prototype for a week. Then a twenty-eight day test on a hundred thousand learners decides the rollout: active days must rise by at least five percent, with learning guardrails flat. The prototype, the model and the deck are all in the submission.
IMPACT MODEL AND SOURCES · PUBLIC VIEW

Base case: +6.63M daily learners (+10.2%)

DAU = monthly learners × active days a month ÷ 30. Party is credited only for turns on days a learner would otherwise skip. Existing learners add 8.5 points (family games 7.1, open tables 1.4); invited relatives add 1.7 points. Party is one game with three ways in: family already on Duolingo, an adult open table for learners with no family on the app, or a relative they invite. Family play alone gives 7.1%; reaching 10% needs all three. There is little headroom: the goal holds only if adoption stays within 2% of plan, so the 28-day test checks adoption and extra active days before any scale-up, and retention is gated at the 25% stage.
